Where ED Wait Time Actually Comes From

Clinical Operations · Real CDC data

16,025 real emergency department visits from the CDC/NCHS 2022 national survey, weighted to all 155.4M US ED visits that year. Set out to find when the ED is worst; the data said that is the wrong question.

  • Between-hospital variation in median door-to-provider time (2–74 min) is 3.9× larger than between-hour variation (11–22 min) — site practice, not the clock, drives the wait.
  • Quantified boarding as the recoverable capacity item: 48.6M ED bay-hours lost per year nationally, median 62 min but 460 min at p90.
  • Implemented the ultimate-cluster variance estimator with Taylor linearisation per CDC guidance — which showed the hypothesised peak-hour effect does not clear α = 0.01. Reported as a negative result rather than dropped.
  • Record layout is derived by parsing CDC's 268-page documentation PDF rather than hardcoding byte offsets; weighting self-validates against CDC's published national total or the build refuses to run.

30-Day Readmission Risk — An Honest Evaluation

Population Health · Real patient data

99,343 real inpatient encounters across 130 US hospitals with a genuine readmission label. The model reaches AUC 0.664 — and the whole point is that AUC is the wrong number to judge it on.

  • Working the top 20% of discharges catches 37.7% of all readmissions at 1.89× enrichment, number-needed-to-screen 4.8 — a capacity-derived risk board, back-tested, predicted vs observed agreeing within a point in every tier.
  • Benchmarked the model against a one-variable rule a nurse can read off the chart: prior inpatient count already gets 32.2%. Gradient boosting buys +5.5pp — real, and far smaller than an AUC comparison implies.
  • Showed class_weight='balanced' destroys calibration — mean predicted risk 46% against an observed 11%. Ranking is unaffected, so an AUC-only evaluation never notices.
  • Measured patient-level leakage rather than assuming it: 23% of patients recur, but grouping the split moves AUC by only +0.002. Grouped split kept because it is correct; inflation reported because it was tested.

Claims Denials & Appeals — Where the Recoverable Money Is

Revenue Cycle · Real CMS data

Three years of CMS Transparency in Coverage filings — 15,545 plan-years, 624 issuer-years, 126.9 million denied claims across every Qualified Health Plan on the federal Marketplace.

  • Of 126.9M denials, 0.168% were ever appealed — fewer than 2 in 1,000 — and 40.1% of appeals were overturned. The binding constraint on recovery is capacity to file, not merit.
  • The payer-reported denial taxonomy is unusable for root-cause work: “Other” is 71.7% of categorised denials and growing yearly, so triage has to be built from provider-side CARC/RARC detail instead.
  • Denial rate is an organisational property, not a market one — 6.0% at p10 to 48.3% max across issuers, with the worst clustered in one parent’s brands and several moving +12 to +16pp in a single year.
  • Handled CMS suppression correctly: ** means withheld because the cell was small, which is missing-not-at-random — averaging the survivors biases every aggregate upward.

Clinical Quality Measures — A Declarative Engine

Population Health · HEDIS / eCQM

HEDIS and CMS eCQM measures over a 13,810-patient EHR — 820,993 encounters, 10.8M observations. Measures are YAML specs, not code, so an annual steward update is a diff a clinical analyst can sign off rather than a code change.

  • CMS122 scores 17.3% poor control — but decomposing the numerator shows 1 patient failed on a recorded result and 203 were never tested. That is a testing gap, not a control problem, and the two need different interventions.
  • Stratification exposes what the average hides: blood pressure control is 57% at ages 18–44 vs 89% at 75+. A 66.9% aggregate conceals a 32-point gap.
  • Guardrails against silent failure — value sets that match no data raise instead of returning 0/0, and every threshold measure reports whether the data can even reach it.
  • Deliverable is the care-gap list: 4,439 gaps across 3,454 patients, 915 with two or more — the outreach list worth building first.

Experimentation Toolkit — What Goes Wrong in A/B Tests

Experimentation · Causal inference

Five failure modes that quietly break online experiments, each measured against known ground truth rather than asserted. Every claim is a measured rejection rate or bias — change a parameter and watch the conclusion move.

  • Peeking at a null experiment 10 times drives the false positive rate to 18.5% against a nominal 5%. Implemented O’Brien–Fleming and always-valid mSPRT bounds, and quantified what each costs in power.
  • CUPED variance reduction matches the ρ² prediction to a tenth of a point; at ρ = 0.7 it is worth 1.96× the traffic, free. Unbiasedness verified over 300 replications.
  • Built an experiment where treatment wins in every segment and loses 41% overall — and the global sample-ratio check passes cleanly because the skew cancels in the total. Run SRM per segment.
  • Closed-form sample sizes are validated by simulation: request 80% power, measure 80.1%. A formula never checked against an empirical rejection rate is not a result.
